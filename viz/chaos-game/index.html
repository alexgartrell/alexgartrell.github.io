Why this works

Jumping halfway toward corner A shrinks the whole triangle by ½ toward A. The three shrunken copies tile the triangle except for the middle, and the Sierpinski triangle is the one shape that is exactly its three half-size copies. Each jump halves your distance to that shape, so after about 20 jumps you are on it to within a pixel, and from then on every point lands on it.

Randomness just decides which copy you visit next, and in the long run it visits them all. Other shapes need other ratios: copies of a pentagon just touch at r = 0.618, the golden ratio, and a hexagon at r = 2/3. The Barnsley fern swaps the corners for four tilted, squashed copies, each an affine map picked with its own probability.
