How it works

Every cell looks at its eight neighbours. All cells update at once: a dead cell with exactly three live neighbours comes alive, a live cell with two or three stays alive, and everything else dies of loneliness or overcrowding. With wrap-around on, the grid is a torus, so a glider leaving the right edge comes back on the left.

Some shapes never change (a block), some repeat (a blinker every 2 generations, a pulsar every 3), and some move: a glider reappears one cell diagonally over every 4 generations. The Gosper gun fires a new glider every 30. Life can even simulate a computer, so in general there is no shortcut for predicting what a pattern will do: you have to run it.
