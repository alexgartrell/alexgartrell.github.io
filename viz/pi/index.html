The axis is logarithmic. Every extra correct decimal needs about 100× more darts, because the typical error shrinks as 1.64 / √n.

Why this works

The square has side 2, so its area is 4. The circle has radius 1, so its area is π. A dart landing anywhere in the square at random hits the circle with probability π / 4 and misses it with probability (4 − π) / 4.

Count the misses and solve for π: π ≈ 4 × (1 − outside ÷ darts). This is the same as four times the share of hits. It is a Monte Carlo method: precise answers built from lots of randomness.
